Interfaces > Interface Batch Size

Interface batch sizes deal with the upload and download interfaces.  The batch size is important to the XML interfacing mode and determines how to break large interface files into smaller segments.  Optimizing this value can play an important role in how long your interfaces take to execute.  The correct value depends on a variety of factors and will likely be different for every customer.

The batch size determines how many header records are processed as a single group.  However, it also controls the various related detail records as well since they are considered "children" of the header.  If you have 100 shipment details on each header and have a batch size of 50, you would be interfacing 50 shipments and 5,000 details per batch.  You should take into account your peak header to detail ratio.  If you fail to do so, your peak interface volumes may fail on certain platforms.

Configuring too large of a batch size on 32-bit platforms can cause interfaces to fail.  Internal testing indicates that a maximum of 50,000 detail records can be interfaced per batch.  This is dependent on the 2 GB memory limit of processes on a 32-bit platform.

 Internal testing has indicated that installations on 64-bit platforms can utilize larger batch sizes without the constraint of the 2 GB memory barrier.  Since 64-bit platforms can address much larger amounts of memory, the row limitations are significantly higher.  Batch sizes of 5,000 and 10,000 have been successfully tested internally without problems.  These large batch sizes also showed improved performance over smaller batch sizes on the 64-bit platforms.  Similar performance gains were observed on 32-bit, but the memory limitations began to play a significant role.
