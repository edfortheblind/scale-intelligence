
        
        <h1 data-source-node="n56"><a name="kanchor567" data-source-node="n57"></a>Dock Management Process Summary...</h1>
        <p data-source-node="n58"><b data-source-node="n59">** Functionality **</b>
        </p>
        <p data-source-node="n60"><a href="0c77698ccfe7d7e04d05ab3b30be92ac8b6430702a653276c2e4e15a459fe918.html" style="font-style: normal;font-weight: bold" data-source-node="n61">Process Flows</a>
        </p>
        <p data-source-node="n62"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n63"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n64">Summary...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n65">Dock management represents several processes, all of which concern tracking 
 shipments after they have arrived in the shipping dock area of the warehouse. These processes 
 include the consolidation of items/containers at the packing area location, 
 the placing of containers on the shipping dock at staging locations, and the loading of containers onto 
 the truck, referred to in the system as dock door locations. These different types of dock locations are specific inventory-tracked areas on or near the shipping dock where a shipment's containers 
 can be assigned to. Each dock management process can be independently 
 switched on/off, thereby allowing a warehouse to use any or all of this 
 functionality. </p>
        <p data-source-node="n66"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n67">View the <a href="0c77698ccfe7d7e04d05ab3b30be92ac8b6430702a653276c2e4e15a459fe918.html" data-source-node="n68">process flow diagram</a> to see a typical layout of a shipping dock that uses SCALE's 
 dock management functionality.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n69"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n70"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n71"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n72">The following are key features of the dock management functional area:</p>
        <p data-source-node="n73"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n74"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n75">Dock Location Structure (Areas vs. Positions)...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n76">Each dock location is composed of two components: a dock location area and a number of dock location positions. A dock location area represents a general section of the shipping dock, including all of the specific positions of a certain dock location. A dock location position represents the specific spots that a dock location area is divided into.  Imagine a taped off section of the shipping dock floor, where the taped-off section is shaped like a grid. The whole grid represents the dock location area, while each small box that makes up the grid represents a position. 
 
</p>
        <p data-source-node="n77"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n78">The system can assign a shipment's container to either a general dock location area or a specific position, depending on what is selected during the dock location selection/assignment process (see the process flows for information on how the system makes this decision). A user can also manually assign containers to an area/position via the Dock Manager Window or the Shipping Container Insight.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n79"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n80"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n81"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n82">Consolidation...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n83">Consolidation represents the step after putaway, when you can locate a shipment's item(s) or shipping container(s) to your warehouse's packing location (before it goes through the packing/close container process). The packing location is typically a place at or near a packing station where the system delivers shipment details/shipping containers after the putaway step. This is made up of consolidation areas and locations, which would represent either an actual packing station or an area on the floor near a packing station. Consolidation locations typically consist of one area and one or several position(s). When a detail/container from a shipment is assigned to a certain consolidation location, then the system will assign all of the shipment's detail(s)/container(s) to this consolidation location.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n84"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n85">Note that SCALE currently supports only one packing location per warehouse, so this should represent your packing area as a whole, and not your specific packing stations.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n86"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n87"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n88"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n89">Staging...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n90">Staging occurs after a container has been packed and closed, when it is placed on a specific spot (represented in SCALE as a staging location) on the shipping dock, in anticipation of being put on a specific carrier's trailer. A staging location is the place on the shipping dock where shipping containers can be placed until it is time for them to be shipped out of the warehouse. Staging locations typically consist of one area and a number of positions. </p>
        <p data-source-node="n91"> </p>
        <h5 data-source-node="n92">Pack and Hold Locations</h5>
        <p data-source-node="n93">Pack and hold locations are staging locations that have been branded (by location name) as a “pack and hold” location. You would transfer containers to these locations that have been packed, but need to be held because their shipment is scheduled for a later delivery date. When ready to ship, containers that reside in a P&amp;H location then can be transferred to a staging location so it can be loaded onto a trailer. </p>
        <p data-source-node="n94"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n95"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n96"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n97">Loading...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n98">Loading occurs when a dock worker places a container onto a carrier's trailer (represented in SCALE as a dock door location). A dock door location represents the trailer that is currently parked at the dock door. Therefore, each dock door typically is represented in SCALE as one dock location area with no positions.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n99"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n100"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n101"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n102">Dock Manager Window...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n103">The Dock Manager Window provides a high-level, overhead view of the dock locations on your warehouse's shipping dock. Using this window, workers can perform several actions to better manage the shipping containers residing in staging areas, or the loads that are being shipped from your dock doors. This window also provides users with detailed information regarding these loads/shipments/containers, which can be used to research activity on your shipping dock. </p>
        <p data-source-node="n104"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n105">To read descriptions of the four main sections of the Dock Manager Window (gauges, data grids, staging areas, and dock doors), see the topic <a href="01a557bc291612fca0d6350cf5de65e550888ef1bdffbf012f81b9fc22c8d55d.html" data-source-node="n106">Using the Dock Manager Window</a>.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n107"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n108"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n109"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n110">Two Approaches In Using Dock Management:  Work and No Work...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n111"> </p>
        <h5 data-source-node="n112">Approach 1: Dock Management Using Work (Creating Next Moves)</h5>
        <p data-source-node="n113">The first dock management approach that SCALE supports is using work to facilitate the movement of containers to and around the shipping dock. This allows the container movement to be tightly controlled by having each step be represented by a work instruction. Each destination location of the container is assigned by the system, and work for the move to that location is then subsequently created.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n114"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n115">When a worker performs a transfer of a container/shipment to a dock location, the system needs to create the work that will be executed by the worker to perform the physical transfer between dock locations. The Dock Management Flow configuration has a Create Next Move setting that determines whether or not work will be created for a particular dock location subclass. The system references this setting during the close container or RF putaway processes only. For all other dock location transfers, the system always creates work.
</p>
        <p data-source-node="n116"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n117">The creation of this “next move” is based on status and location subclass (packing or staging) as defined on the flow detail. Typically, you would setup your flow to create a next move when a container is in a Loading Pending status (e.g. the status that is tied to your staging location subclass). Note that you cannot create a next move for the dock door location subclass.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n118"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n119">Note that if a situation arises where a worker still needs to manually decide where a container should be on the shipping dock, they can still use the Dock Manager Window to move the container (and the system will update/create the work).</p>
        <p data-source-node="n120"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n121"> </p>
        <h5 data-source-node="n122">Approach 2: Dock Management Not Using Work (Manual Transfers)</h5>
        <p data-source-node="n123">An alternative approach to using dock management in your warehouse is to not use work execution to facilitate the movement of containers to and around the shipping dock. Workers then would use the RF's Immediate Dock Transfer screen to move containers from dock locations such as packing location, staging locations, and dock doors. The worker gets to choose exactly to what location they are taking the container, unless the container's load is already assigned to a dock door. Note that if a container is associated with open or in process work, transfers cannot be performed using this screen. It is important to note that if you want to use this "no work" approach, you need to make sure that the Create Next Move setting is not selected on your dock management flow record (for any status for which you wish to use "immediate" dock transfers without work).</p>
