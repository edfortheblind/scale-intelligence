The following is a discussion of how labor plans can be used in your warehouse:

 

- Calculating Time Based On Unit of Measure

- Calculating Time Based On Quantity

 

 

For the following examples, suppose workers are picking eaches (EA) and cases (CS) picking from different areas of the warehouse. Within the EA picking, there is one particular customer whose picks are done differently than the rest of the customers (making them more time consuming). You would then need to create the following labor groups to address this:

 

Labor Group: Each Picking (General)

- Priority: 1

- Filter: Customer != ‘X’ (where X is my special customer)

- Full Time Employees: 5

- Hours Per Full Time Employee: 8

- Temporary Employees: 2

- Hours Per Temporary Employee: 4

- Quantity Units of Measure: EA

- Estimated Time Per Transaction: 2 minutes

 

Labor Group: Each Picking (CustomerX)

- Priority: 2

- Filter: Customer = ‘X’

- Full Time Employees: 2

- Hours Per Full Time Employee: 8

- Temporary Employees: 0

- Hours Per Temporary Employee: 4

- Quantity Units of Measure: EA

- Estimated Time Per Transaction: 3 minutes

 

Labor Group: Case Picking

- Priority: 3

- Filter: All Shipments

- Full Time Employees: 5

- Hours Per Full Time Employee: 8

- Temporary Employees: 4

- Hours Per Temporary Employee: 4

- Quantity Units of Measure: CS

- Estimated Time Per Transaction: 4 minutes

 

 

Example: Calculating Time Based On Unit Of Measure

Suppose all of the items have a case pack of 10. Also, that there are 1,000 shipments for non-CustomerX (each with 2 lines). The quantity on each line is 12. Additionally, there are 100 shipments for customer X (each with 2 lines). On each shipment, one of the lines has a quantity of 12, and the other has a quantity of 20.

 

When the labor plan runs, it will start with the first priority group, which is the Each Picking (General group). It will select all of the lines for non-CustomerX, which is the 2,000 lines for the 1,000 shipments. Since every line has a quantity of 12 (and the items have a case pack of 10), that means every line has EA demand (the quantity of eaches does not matter, as it takes roughly the same time to pick a quantity of 2 as a quantity of 1 from a single location). Therefore, you have 2,000 EA transactions. It takes this times 2 minutes per transaction for a total of 4,000 minutes (or 66.67 hours) for this group.

 

The next group is Each Picking (CustomerX). For all of the lines with a quantity of 12, there is an EA demand. The lines with quantity 20 are even cases, so there is no EA demand on those. Therefore, there are 100 transactions for eaches for CustomerX. It takes that times 3 minutes per transaction for a total of 300 minutes (or 5 hours).

 

The final group is the Case Picking group. This group selects all shipment details. In the non-CustomerX shipments, all 2,000 lines have quantities greater than 10, so they all have CS demand. Similarly, all 200 lines for CustomerX have CS demand. That’s a total of 2,200 transactions. It takes that times 4 minutes to get 8,800 minutes (or 146.67 hours), for this group.

 

 

 

 

 

Example: Calculating Time Based On Quantity  

If the above groups were configured to have a calculate time based on set to quantity, the system will count each quantity as a transaction. Suppose all of the items have a case pack of 10. 1,000 shipments are for non-CustomerX, each with 2 lines. The quantity on each line is 12. Additionally, there are 100 shipments for CustomerX, each with 2 lines. On each shipment, one of the lines has a quantity of 12 and the other has a quantity of 20.

 

When the labor plan runs, it will start with the first priority group, which is the Each Picking (General group). It will select all of the lines for non-CustomerX, which is the 2,000 lines for the 1,000 shipments. Since every line has a quantity of 12 and the items have a case pack of 10, that means every line has EA demand (2 eaches per line). Since the calculate time based on is set to quantity, you have to consider every EA a transaction. Therefore, you have 4,000 EA transactions. It takes this times 2 minutes per transaction for a total of 8,000 minutes (or 133.33 hours) for this group.

 

The next group is Each Picking (CustomerX). For all of the lines with a quantity of 12, there is EA demand. The lines with quantity 20 are even cases so there is no EA demand on those. Again, the calculate time based on is set to quantity. Therefore, there are 100 details each with a quantity of 2 EA so 200 transactions for Customer X. It takes that times 3 minutes per transaction for a total of 600 minutes (or 10 hours).

 

The final group is the Case Picking group. This group selects all shipment details. In the non-CustomerX shipments, all 2,000 lines have quantities greater than 10, so they all have single CS demand. Which is 2,000 transactions. Similarly, all 200 lines for CustomerX have CS demand. The second detail of these records have a quantity of 20 which means 2 transactions. That’s a total of 2,300 transactions. It takes that times 4 minutes to get 9,200 minutes, (or 153.33 hours) for this group.
