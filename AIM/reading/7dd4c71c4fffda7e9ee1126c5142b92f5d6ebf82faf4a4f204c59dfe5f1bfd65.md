        <h4 data-source-node="n67">Summary...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n68">Labor management tracks user productivity throughout the warehouse. 
 The system generates this labor data when a worker performs one of many 
 warehouse actions, such as work execution (both full-screen and RF), packing, 
 and receiving. Labor management can provide you with such information 
 as "how efficient is this employee at packing?" or "how 
 many containers can this employee pick an hour?" It also provides how well the labor plan measures up to what was expected. You can use this 
 data to plan your warehouse operations. </p>

        <h4 data-source-node="n84">Using Labor Management Data...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n85">The system includes few options to view labor data on both individuals and teams: 
 Labor Activity Insight, and Labor Monitoring . You can also 
 use the Manual Labor Entry Screen  to manually enter labor data records that are not "tracked" 
 by the application in typical warehouse processing. For example, you can 
 have your custodial staff log their time used in cleaning the warehouse, 
 labeling this time with a custom work type used only for these type of 
 tasks.</p>

        <h4 data-source-node="n89">How Are Labor Management Records Generated?...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n90">When an employee executes a warehouse action (such as confirming a work 
 instruction), the system generates a request record. This request is processed 
 by the Labor Management service (which runs continuously, looking 
 for labor requests). A labor management detail record then is generated 
 by this service to represent the action that the user performed. These 
 detail records can be viewed in the Labor Activity Insight.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n91"> </p>

        <h4 data-source-node="n94">Labor Plans...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n95">Labor plans identify how the system will calculate estimated labor data during the run wave process. An actual plan is a record that determines which labor group(s) to calculate values for in the wave, and in what sequence order to process them. Note that the labor plan execution wave step must be a part of your flow to use this value.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n96"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n97"> </p>
        <p data-source-node="n98"> </p>
        <h4 data-source-node="n99">Labor Groups...</h4>
        <p data-source-node="n100">A labor group represents a collection of warehouse users that perform related work duties, such as picking product for a certain company's shipments. The labor group records in SCALE define such employee information as how many people (full time and temporary) who work at your warehouse, as well as what quantity units of measure they typically process.</p>

        <h4 data-source-node="n104">Direct/Indirect Labor...</h4>
        <ul data-source-node="n105">
            <li data-source-node="n106">Direct Labor describes a activity performed by a worker that provides direct value to the warehouse. For example, such activities as performing actions in SCALE, or such physical activities as loading shipments onto a truck.<br data-source-node="n107"><br data-source-node="n108"></li>
            <li data-source-node="n109">Indirect Labor describes a basic maintenance activity performed by a worker that is required for the business to operate, but does not directly impact the customer. For example, activities such as cleaning the warehouse.</li>
        </ul>
