
        
        <h1 style="text-align: left;font-size: 12pt" data-source-node="n57">Wave Picking Group</h1>
        <p data-source-node="n58">A wave picking group defines the number of loose or full containers that can be included in a group, as well as the specific locations or work zones where the group can be used. It serves as a physical tool to streamline and consolidate the picking process within a warehouse.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n59">Common types of picking groups include:</p>
        <ul data-source-node="n60">
            <li data-source-node="n61">
                <p data-source-node="n62">Carts</p>
            </li>
            <li data-source-node="n63">
                <p data-source-node="n64">Cherry pickers</p>
            </li>
            <li data-source-node="n65">
                <p data-source-node="n66">Forklifts</p>
            </li>
        </ul>
        <p data-source-node="n67">During a wave, the system can execute the group picking step, assigning containers (created during the container creation step) to the appropriate picking group based on configuration.</p>
        <p data-source-node="n68"> </p>

                    <li data-source-node="n93">
                        <p data-source-node="n94">Unlike loose containers, full cases are shipped in their original storage container and not repacked, allowing them to be stacked and increasing the number a pick group can handle.</p>
                    </li>
