Trading Partner Management Process Summary

** Functionality **

 

 

Summary...

Trading Partner Management is a website that acts as a supply chain collaboration tool between the customer and the application. It allows for real-time access of shipment status information over the Internet. You can enter orders for items over the web, as well as see the status of these orders. Also, this website can be configured to reflect the image of any company located in your warehouse. This allows you to preserve the multi-company environment of your warehouse. The TPM site includes five main functional areas: Supplier Enablement (purchase order, shipment entry), Order Management (order entry, status), Statistics (inbound, item, outbound), and Inventory Management (inventory status). You can access these from the TPM Main Menu.

The following are key features of the trading partner management functional area:

 

 

Web Users...

A web user is a person who logs onto Trading Partner Management. There are two types of users: external or internal. External users can only access their own shipment/receipt information. Examples of external users are people not employed by your organization, including a customer, business partner, or supplier. External users can access either a single company or a select group of companies. Internal users, however, can access any shipment/receipt information in the system. Examples of internal users are employees working offsite or using the company intranet.

 

 

 

Single Company vs. Multi Company Processing...

Trading Partner Management can be configured to reflect the image of any company located in your warehouse. If your warehouse supports multiple companies, you can define the appearance of your site to reflect each company supported in your warehouse. If you are a single-company warehouse, you can define the appearance of the website to reflect this. The system can display your warehouse's name on the login page, and a warehouse graphic on the main menu.

As shown in the graphic, each web user can see a different version of the Trading Partner Management website, depending on their user type and company access:

  • Internal User, Multi-Company Access: The Warehouse Manager works for Big Warehouse. His job is to oversee the work done for both companies, Mountain Auto and Gridiron Recreation. In this example, the manager needs to check the status of some shipments in the warehouse. When he logs onto Trading Partner Management, he will see the Big Warehouse version of the website. He will be able to access all the shipment information for both companies.

     

  • External User, Single-Company Access: Frank's Garage is a customer of Mountain Auto. In this example, they have ordered some product from Mountain Auto and want to check the shipment's status. When a Frank's Garage representative logs onto TPM, he will see the Mountain Auto version of the website. He will only be able to access the garage's Mountain Auto shipment information.

     

  • External User, Multi-Company Access: GoMart is a customer of both Mountain Auto and Gridiron Recreation. In this example, they have ordered product from both companies, and want to check the status of the order. When a GoMart representative logs onto TPM, they will see the Big Warehouse version of the website. He will be able to access his shipment information from both Mountain Auto and Gridiron Recreation, respectively.

 

Remember that external users have access only to their own order(s). A customer ID is required to view any order information. Internal users, however, have access to any order(s) in the system. No customer ID is required to view order information.

 

 

 

Purchase Orders...

Purchase orders indicate what item(s) your warehouse should be expecting to arrive at the receiving dock. PO's are used by a warehouse's suppliers/vendors to supply receipt information ahead of the arrival of the truck to their warehouse. You can create and manage purchase orders on the TPM website. For example, suppose your warehouse's supplier is planning on sending you 500 pallets of Item A over the course of a year. They would first create a purchase order for Item A, adding a quantity of 500. When they are ready to ship some of Item A to your warehouse, they would create a shipment associated with this PO, which would indicate how many of the item they are going to send out. When the year is over, and all the Item A quantity has been shipped, the supplier would close the purchase order.

 

 

 

Order Management...

The order status component can be used to research the status of orders entered via the Order Entry module. This is helpful if you want to view the current status of your order in the warehouse. For information on the web order entry component, see the Order Entry Process Summary topic.

 

 

 

Web Statistics...

The web statistics component can be used to research your warehouse's receipts, inventory, and shipments. Users will enter search criteria information, and the system will return summary data statistics that match the criteria. The system can output these statistics in several formats, such as HTML and XML.

 

 

 

Inventory Status...

The inventory status component can be used to research the inventory status of items in a warehouse. Users will enter criteria to search for an item. The system will then display the appropriate item(s), and whether they are available in the warehouse's inventory. This is helpful if you want to check if an item is in stock before placing an order.

 

 

 

Email Alerts...

An email alert is a message the system sends to a customer when a warehouse event occurs. The purpose of the message is to inform a customer as to the status of their shipment. The system triggers alerts if a shipment meets a certain system status, or if the system generates a certain piece of documentation. Alerts are defined in the Email Alerts Window. In this window, you can also indicate what email address you want the alert to be sent, and the template you want the system to use to generate the email.
