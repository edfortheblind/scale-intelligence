Calculating Shipment 1 - One LTL Class

In the shipment 1 example above, the system looks at the effective date of the rate base and the origin and destination postal codes. It assigns rate base RD01.

 

Next it looks at the LTL class (50) and the weight of the shipment (600 pounds) at that class. It uses the rate for '501-1000' pounds. The rate per hundredweight is $42.50. It multiples the weight 6.00 X 42.50 = $255.00. It then compares this to the minimum charge of $110.25. Since this charge is more than the minimum, it will use $255.00 as the freight charge. If the calculated amount is less than the minimum charge, it would use the minimum charge. If there is a minimum charge on both the header and detail levels, the detail minimum charge is used. If there is minimum charge is not defined at the detail level, the header level amount is used.

 

 

Calculating Shipment 2 - Multiple LTL Classes 

In the shipment 2 example above, the system looks at the effective date of the rate base and the origin and destination postal codes. It assigns rate base RD01.

 

When there are mixed LTL classes on a shipment, each class is rated separately and then totaled to determine the total freight charge on the shipment. Because the total shipment weight is still 600 pounds, the '501-1000' pounds rates are used. However, the individual rates per class are used.

 

Class 50: 42.50 X 4.00 = $170.00

Class 55: 41.75 X 2.00 = $83.50

Total Freight Charge = $170.00 + 83.50 = $253.50

 

It then compares this to the minimum charge of $110.25. Since this charge is more than the minimum, it will use the $253.50 freight charge.

 

 

 

 

Deficit Weight Rating...

 

During deficit weight rating, an additional check is performed on the rate. During deficit rating, the shipper cannot be charged more than what the minimum would be at the next weight break. The next weight in the examples above would be 1000.01 pounds. So, in these examples, the system would calculate the minimum charge at the weight break of 1000.01 pounds which is $41.80. It would take 10.00 X 41.80 = $418.00. Since this is more than the actual freight charge in Shipment 1 above, the charge remains the lower amount of $255.00.

 

When performing deficit weight rating on a mixed class shipment, the weight break of the lowest class on the shipment is used. In Shipment 2, it would use LTL Class 50. Since the $418.00 is more than the $253.50 calculated freight charge, the lower amount is used.

 

 

 

Freight All Kinds (FAK)...

 

The purpose of FAK rating is to receive a cheaper rate by rating the product at the least expensive LTL class within a range. This agreement between the carrier and the shipper allows the shipment to be rated at a specified agreed upon class, no matter what the actual LTL class of the items are on the shipment. This make the rating calculation process much easier and typically results in cheaper rates for the shipper. For example, if you ship products in LTL classes 70, 85 and 100, you may negotiate with your carrier to rate everything at LTL class '85'. The system would just look at the weight and weight break charge at one LTL class.
